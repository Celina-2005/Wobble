## Discovery Phase – Problem Exploration and Concept Selection (September – October)
The Discovery Phase combines a divergent and convergent research step with the aim of defining the core problem and establishing an initial product direction.
In September, a **literature review (N=8)** was conducted on stressors in raising young children. The focus was on problem areas such as emotional escalation, parental overload, and self-regulation in young children. This divergent step resulted in a broad overview of potential sources of stress.
In October, a **field study as survey (N=21)** followed with parents of children aged 2 to 5. This convergent step aimed to determine which stressor from the literature review was perceived as most dominant by the target group. The combination of literature and field data led to a well-founded problem definition and the selection of a single central focus. The phase concluded with a concept pitch, presenting the selected product concept.


## Definition Phase – Concept Delineation and Validation (November – January)
The Definition Phase focuses on deepening and testing the chosen concept. In November, a divergent brainstorming phase was initiated based on the design requirements from Discovery. This phase explored key factors, interaction principles, and potential solutions. Parallel to this, test protocols were developed for field research.
In December, **field studies as waves (N=29)** were conducted on emotion skills and emotion recognition in children aged 2 to 5. This convergent phase had less relevant data than expected, mostly in the colourtest. Therefore, a supplementary, divergent literature review was conducted to fill in the gaps and strengthen the concept's foundation withing the colour analysis (N=2). So the focus in the definition phase fell the focus on formulating the design requirements for future prototyping. 

## Develop 1 – Expert Input and Focus Definition (February)
The Develop Phase started with a divergent exploration of the Wobble concept through a comprehensive mind map, mapping all potential features, interaction types, and technological components. This exercise revealed that the concept in its entirety was too broad and required prioritization.
To support this, a **literature review** on relevant technologies and their role in child development and parenting was conducted. In parallel, **expert interviews** were carried out with Liezl Mullebrouck and Hanne Corne. These provided insights into emotional development, sensory stimulation, and appropriate interaction strategies for young children.
The findings from literature and expert input were synthesized into a **MoSCoW prioritization matrix**, defining the scope for further development. Key focus areas included haptic breathing guidance, sound as a trigger, and comforting touch interactions. Less critical features (Could/Won’t) were excluded to maintain feasibility within the project timeframe.

## Develop 2 – Sensory Testing and Material Exploration (March – April)
In this phase, a convergent, experimental approach was taken to validate key interaction principles. A functional prototype was developed to test breathing guidance **through sensory feedback**.
The study investigated :
- The effect of mechanical breathing rhythms (moving surface) on calming children,
- The influence of auditory breathing cues,
- The combined effect of movement and sound.

Additionally, different materials and interaction forms (e.g. textile, silicone, felt) were explored to evaluate comfort, engagement, and perceived safety.
Results indicate that **haptic movement** is the most effective calming mechanism, as it provides a tangible focus that supports unconscious regulation. Audio alone was less effective, and synchronized breathing (movement + sound) proved too complex without further tuning to children’s natural breathing pace.

## Evolution of Prototypes
Within semester 1, prototyping was limited to **conceptual and low-fidelity explorations**, such as sketches, scenarios, and cardboard models. Physical and interactive prototypes will be further developed and tested in semester 2, building on the insights gained in semester 1.
